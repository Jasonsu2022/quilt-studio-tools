How the Quilt Backing & Batting Calculator Works

First, we add your chosen overage to all four sides of your quilt top (Wt = W + 2 × O, Lt = L + 2 × O). Domestic machine or hand quilting typically needs 3 inches per side (6 inches total), while longarm quilting frames require at least 4 inches per side (8 inches total) for clamps and leaders.

Next, we subtract a 2-inch selvage allowance from your bolt width to find your usable fabric width (for example, 44" quilting cotton gives 42" usable width). If your target backing fits within one usable width, you only need a single piece. Otherwise, we calculate the panels needed in both directions using a sturdy 1/2" seam allowance (1" net width lost per seam) and automatically choose the layout that uses the least fabric—unless you lock Directional Print to keep patterns upright.

Standard 44" Cotton vs. 108" Wideback Fabric

Standard 42"–44" quilting cotton is easy to match to your quilt top and widely available, but any quilt wider than 42" after overage requires piecing two or more panels. Using 108" wideback eliminates seams completely on most Throw, Twin, and Queen quilts, saving piecing time and creating a smoother back—just remember that oversized King quilts (over 106" finished) still need one seam even with 108" wideback.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much overage do I need for longarm vs. domestic quilting?
Most longarm quilters require at least 4 inches of extra backing and batting on every side (adding 8 inches total to both width and length) so the quilt can be pinned or clamped onto the frame leaders. If you are basting and quilting on a domestic sewing machine or by hand, 3 inches per side (6 inches total) is usually plenty.
When is 108" wideback worth buying?
For Throw, Twin, and Queen quilts, 108" wideback lets you cut a seamless single-piece backing with zero piecing and often uses less total yardage than piecing three widths of 44" cotton. Only oversized King quilts whose backing target exceeds 106" on both sides still require one seam on 108" fabric.
What does Directional Print Fabric do?
When your backing fabric has a one-way print (like trees, text, or animals), turning panels sideways to save yardage would make the print lie on its side. Enabling Directional Print locks the calculator to vertical seams so every panel runs upright along the quilt length.
How do I choose the right pre-packaged batting size?
Compare your minimum batting size (quilt top plus your overage on all four sides) against standard package dimensions. A package can be rotated 90 degrees if needed. If a Queen quilt top (90" × 96") needs 4" longarm overage per side (98" × 104"), a 90" × 108" Queen package is too narrow—step up to a 120" × 120" King package.
Why does the calculator deduct 2" for selvages and use 1/2" seams?
Woven selvage edges are tighter than the rest of the fabric and can pucker after washing, so trimming about 1" from each edge (2" total) keeps your backing flat. Joining backing panels with a generous 1/2" seam allowance (1" net loss per seam) pressed open creates a strong, low-bulk seam that handles quilting tension safely.
